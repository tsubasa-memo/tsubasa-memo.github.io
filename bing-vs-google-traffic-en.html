What Bing Webmaster Tools Revealed About AI Search
— Why Bing Traffic Was 3x Higher Than Google on My Site

Published: August 7, 2026

Hi, I'm Tsubasa.

Bing was sending three times more traffic to my site than Google. I discovered this only after registering my site with Bing Webmaster Tools and comparing the data against Google Search Console.

Bing Webmaster Tools is a free site management tool from Microsoft. It shows how your site appears in Bing search results and, more importantly, how it's cited by Copilot's AI-generated responses. It offers functionality comparable to Google Search Console, plus an "AI Performance" feature that tracks Copilot citations — something Search Console doesn't provide at the same level of detail.

I registered my site and compared data from both tools over the same three-month period. The results were unexpected. Google sent 35 clicks; Bing sent 94. Bing was delivering roughly three times more actual visitors. Impressions told a different story — Google showed 3,460 and Bing showed 885 — so by raw impression count, Google appeared dominant. But the people who actually arrived at my site came overwhelmingly from Bing.

This is a small personal site with around 100 articles, hosted on GitHub Pages. Large media outlets or major e-commerce sites may see different results. But at least for my site, the assumption that "Google is the only search engine that matters" was already wrong. This article examines why this happened and what Bing's index means now that AI search is mainstream.

Bing Sent 94 Clicks, Google Sent 35 — What Was Happening on My Site

Here are the numbers. The following is a side-by-side comparison of Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools data for the same three-month period.

Metric Google Bing
Impressions 3,460 885
Clicks 35 94
CTR 1.0% 10.6%

By impressions, Google is roughly four times higher. But by clicks — the number of people who actually visited the site — Bing is 2.7 times higher. The CTR difference is more than tenfold.

"More impressions = more people seeing my content" is an easy assumption to make, but it's wrong. Search Console's impression count includes a large number of displays that never actually reached a user's eyes. The next section explains why.

Why Search Console Impression Numbers Are Misleading

Search Console counts an impression every time a page appears in search results — even at position 42 or position 92.

Position 42 is page five of Google's search results. Position 92 is page ten. Virtually nobody browses past page two. Pages five and ten might as well not exist.

In my Search Console data, queries like these appeared:

  • "seoイノベーション 低価格500円 seo対策" (a Japanese SEO query) — position 42, 442 impressions, 0 clicks
  • "seo 外注" (SEO outsourcing) — position 88, 75 impressions, 0 clicks
  • "スマホで証明写真" (ID photo on smartphone) — position 92, 26 impressions, 0 clicks

These three queries alone accounted for 543 impressions — about 16% of the total 3,460 — all from positions where no human would ever see them.

Bing, by contrast, tends not to display pages for loosely relevant queries. Its impression count is lower, but the click-through rate on displayed results is much higher. Bing's 885 impressions are closer to the actual number of times a real user saw my site in search results.

When comparing search engine contribution, use clicks, not impressions. Impression count is just the number of times Google's algorithm listed your page as a candidate — it says nothing about whether anyone saw it.

Being in Bing's Index Means Exposure Across Copilot, ChatGPT Search, Perplexity, and DuckDuckGo

Bing's index doesn't only serve Bing.com. It functions as the information source for Microsoft Copilot, ChatGPT Search, Perplexity, and DuckDuckGo. Being properly indexed by Bing means potential exposure across all of these AI search services.

"Bing has fewer users than Google — why should I care?" is a reasonable question. Bing's standalone global search share is roughly 5–10%, compared to Google's approximately 89%.

But Bing's index — its searchable database of the web — is not used by Bing.com alone.

Only two companies in the world have built and maintain their own web-scale search indexes: Google and Microsoft (Bing). Building such an index from scratch requires massive technical capability and infrastructure investment. It's not realistic for other search services or AI products to create their own. As a result, most AI search services outside Google's ecosystem rely on Bing's index as their backend.

Microsoft Copilot retrieves information from Bing's index through Microsoft's "Prometheus model" to generate responses.

M Reinventing search with a new AI-powered Microsoft Bing and Edge (February 2023) blogs.microsoft.com

ChatGPT Search also uses Bing's index as a key information source. OpenAI's VP of Engineering, Srinivas Narayanan, stated in an official Reddit AMA: "We use a suite of services, and Bing is an important one among them." On Microsoft's side, Bing's Principal Product Manager Fabrice Canel confirmed at a public event that OpenAI leverages Bing's index for fresh content retrieval. Additionally, engineers have verified that sites penalized in Bing simultaneously disappeared from ChatGPT Search, demonstrating the linkage.

B The Inside Perspective on Modern Search — An Interview with Fabrice Canel of Bing (2024) botify.com S Bing Powers ChatGPT Search (November 2024) seroundtable.com

Perplexity AI's CEO Aravind Srinivas stated in a podcast that the company initially built its system relying on Bing's search index combined with OpenAI's models. While Perplexity is now building its own index, the architecture of using a search engine as the RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) foundation remains.

DuckDuckGo explicitly states in its official help pages: "We primarily source our traditional links and images from Bing."

D Where do DuckDuckGo search results come from? duckduckgo.com

In summary, being indexed by Bing means your content is potentially available to:

  • Microsoft Copilot
  • ChatGPT Search
  • Perplexity
  • DuckDuckGo

Bing's standalone search share may be small, but when you consider the entire AI search ecosystem it supports, the value of being properly indexed by Bing is far greater than the Bing.com traffic numbers alone suggest.

Japan's Desktop Search: Bing Overtook Google — StatCounter Data

StatCounter's May 2026 data shows that in Japan's desktop search market, Bing reached 48.74% share while Google fell to 45.96%. Globally, Bing hovers around 10%, making Japan's desktop market a striking outlier.

S Desktop Search Engine Market Share Japan gs.statcounter.com

The previous month (April 2026) showed an even more pronounced gap in host-level data, with bing.com at 52.32%. Even across all devices (including mobile), Bing holds 33–36% share in Japan, closing in on Google's 55–58%.

Why is Japan such an outlier? The likely explanation lies in Japan's corporate IT culture and Microsoft 365 adoption.

Japan's large enterprises and government agencies overwhelmingly standardize on Windows PCs, Edge browser, and Microsoft 365 as their workplace environment. Edge's default search engine is Bing, so every search from Edge counts as Bing traffic.

More critically, corporate policies around generative AI play a role. Surveys from 2025–2026 indicate that approximately 72% of Japanese companies either prohibit or are considering prohibiting the use of public generative AI tools (including personal ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude accounts) for work purposes.

P Survey on Generative AI Tool Usage in Corporations — Microsoft Copilot Leads Enterprise Adoption (SDE Partners, 2026) prtimes.jp

Under "approved tools only" policies, the available AI depends on which productivity suite the company has adopted. Microsoft 365 organizations get Copilot; Google Workspace organizations get Gemini. In Japan's large enterprises and government agencies, Microsoft 365 adoption significantly exceeds Google Workspace, and Microsoft Copilot leads with 45.3% share among standardized enterprise AI tool deployments. Microsoft 365 Copilot has announced its ISMAP (Japan's government cloud security assessment) application and supports conditional access control via Entra ID (formerly Azure AD), making it adoptable even in organizations with strict security requirements.

This directly translates to search traffic. Copilot's backend is Bing, and all Copilot-initiated searches count as Bing traffic. The more employees in Microsoft 365 organizations use Copilot daily, the more Japan's desktop Bing search share gets pushed up.

A caveat: StatCounter's measurement is heavily influenced by default browser search engine settings. One survey found that approximately 34% of Bing users "are not aware they are using Bing" — they're searching through the Windows taskbar or Edge address bar without conscious choice. Additionally, Edge's prerendering feature (pre-loading the next page) and Copilot's background network communications may inflate Bing's share with pageviews that don't represent actual user behavior, as acknowledged in StatCounter's official FAQ. These numbers don't precisely measure "intentional Bing usage." However, whether the search is intentional or not, the user's query flows through Bing's index and feeds into the AI ecosystem.

Tracking Copilot Citations with Bing Webmaster Tools' AI Performance

Bing Webmaster Tools' "AI Performance" is currently the only tool that quantitatively shows how many times your site has been cited in Copilot's AI-generated responses. Added as a public preview in February 2026, it provides two metrics: citation count (Citations) and the search queries AI used to retrieve information (Grounding Queries).

B Introducing AI Performance in Bing Webmaster Tools Public Preview (February 2026) blogs.bing.com

On my site, the top queries cited by Copilot were:

  • "リサイズとは" (What is resizing) — 427 Citations
  • "レタッチとは" (What is retouching) — 175 Citations
  • "instagram フィード投稿サイズ" (Instagram feed post size) — 31 Citations

The top two are both glossary entries, totaling 602 citations — 68% of the overall 878. Definition and explanation-style content like glossary pages tends to be cited frequently by AI as "grounding" (factual backing) for its responses. Notably, English queries ("top image recoloring services" — 17 citations, "best photo recoloring apps" — 17 citations) also cited my Japanese-language site.

Google Search Console added an AI report in June 2026, but it only shows impression counts — click data, CTR, and query data remain unavailable. For quantitatively understanding "how AI is citing my site," Bing Webmaster Tools is currently the only option. I covered Google Search Console's generative AI report in a separate article (Japanese).

If you want to understand how AI search treats your site, registering with Bing Webmaster Tools is not optional.

Setting Up Bing Webmaster Tools and IndexNow

For those who've read this far and want to check their own site, here are three things to do. All free, and setup takes less than a day.

1. Register Your Site with Bing Webmaster Tools

  1. Go to Bing Webmaster Tools and sign in with a Microsoft account
  2. Add your site URL
  3. Verify ownership (adding a meta tag to your HTML head is the easiest method)
  4. Once registered, check clicks and impressions under "Search Performance" in the left menu
  5. Check Copilot citation data under "AI Performance" in the same menu

If you've registered with Google Search Console before, the process is nearly identical.

2. Set Up IndexNow

IndexNow is a protocol that instantly notifies Bing and Yandex when you publish or update a page. Since Bing's index is the backend for Copilot and ChatGPT Search, faster Bing indexing means faster AI search exposure.

  1. Generate an IndexNow API key (a random string)
  2. Place the key as a text file at your site's root (e.g., https://example.com/abc123.txt)
  3. Send an HTTP POST request to the IndexNow API whenever you update a page

WordPress users can automate this with a plugin. Static sites can use a shell script or Python script. IndexNow does not support Google — for Google, continue using Search Console's URL Inspection tool for individual requests.

Summary

If you only look at Google Search Console to assess search traffic, you're missing Bing-sourced visits. On my site, Bing clicks were roughly three times higher than Google's.

Bing's index serves not just Bing.com but also Microsoft Copilot, ChatGPT Search, Perplexity, and DuckDuckGo as a backend. Being properly indexed by Bing means exposure across all of these AI search services.

In Japan's desktop search market, Bing has overtaken Google at 48.74% share (StatCounter, May 2026) — driven by Microsoft 365 adoption and corporate AI policies that funnel employees toward Copilot. While this specific market dynamic is most pronounced in Japan, the structural relationship between Bing's index and the AI search ecosystem applies globally.

Register with Bing Webmaster Tools, check AI Performance, and set up IndexNow. It's all free. Start by comparing your own Google and Bing click counts.

FAQ

Q. Is Bing Webmaster Tools free?

A. Yes, completely free. If you have a Microsoft account, you just add your site URL and verify ownership. Same model as Google Search Console — no fees whatsoever.

Q. Does IndexNow notify Google as well?

A. No, Google does not participate in the IndexNow protocol. IndexNow notifications reach Bing, Yandex, Seznam, Naver, and other participating search engines only. For Google, continue using Search Console's URL Inspection tool.

Q. Why does Search Console show many impressions but few clicks?

A. Search Console counts an impression every time your page appears in search results — even at position 42 (page five) or position 92 (page ten). Virtually nobody browses that deep. These "invisible impressions" accumulate, inflating the apparent impression count and depressing CTR. Bing's more selective display approach results in fewer impressions but higher click-through rates.

Q. How can I increase my Copilot AI citation count?

A. Structured, definition-rich content tends to get cited more frequently. On my site, glossary pages ("What is resizing," "What is retouching") accounted for 68% of all Copilot citations. Placing a clear definition or conclusion at the beginning of each h2 section, building out FAQ content, and implementing JSON-LD structured data (FAQPage, Article) are practical steps. For more on this approach, see my article on LLMO article writing strategies (Japanese).

Q. When did the AI Performance feature become available?

A. The public preview launched on February 10, 2026. Once your site is registered in Bing Webmaster Tools, "AI Performance" appears in the left menu, showing citation counts and grounding queries for Copilot's AI-generated responses.

Q. Does Google Search Console have an AI report?

A. Google added a "Generative AI Performance Report" to Search Console in June 2026, but it only shows impression counts. Click data, CTR, and query data (which search terms triggered AI citations) are not provided. For detailed AI citation analysis, Bing Webmaster Tools currently offers significantly more data.

Q. Is the Japan desktop search share data relevant outside Japan?

A. The specific market share numbers (Bing 48.74%, Google 45.96%) are unique to Japan's desktop market, driven by high Microsoft 365 adoption in enterprises and government agencies. Other markets won't show the same ratios. However, the structural point — that Bing's index powers Copilot, ChatGPT Search, Perplexity, and DuckDuckGo — applies globally regardless of local market share. Even in markets where Bing's direct search share is 5–10%, its index reaches users through all of these AI services.
