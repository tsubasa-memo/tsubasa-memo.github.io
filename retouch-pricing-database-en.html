Hi, I'm Tsubasa.

When I lined up the rates of 15 photo retouching services in Japan, the gap between the cheapest and the most expensive option for a portrait was more than tenfold. Some charge per spot — ¥100 to remove a single blemish. Others charge per person, per image, or won't publish rates at all. The pricing structures are so different that a simple side-by-side comparison is impossible unless someone normalizes the data first.

That's what this article does. I've gathered the published rates of 15 Japanese retouching and photo-editing services into one page. The short answer: portrait (skin) retouching in Japan mostly falls in the ¥1,000–6,000 per image range, background removal costs tens to hundreds of yen per image, and complex object removal (power lines, etc.) is quoted case by case, typically from a few thousand to tens of thousands of yen.

If background removal is all you need, a separate article covers 11 cutout specialists starting at ¥19 per image: "背景切り抜き・白抜きの代行・外注費用相場｜1枚19円〜11社比較" (Japanese).

All prices in this article are the rates published on each company's official website as of March 2026. Prices change, so confirm the latest figures on the official sites. Note that many services price web-use images and large-format print images differently.

Per-Spot vs. Per-Person: Japanese Retouching Services Count Prices Differently

The single most confusing thing about comparing retouching quotes in Japan is that services count differently: "per spot," "per person," or "per image." A flat comparison across those units doesn't work.

Per-spot services (BELLEFOTO, Remage, Motto, Syazou no Takumi's itemized menu) charge for each fix. Removing one blemish costs a few hundred yen, but fixing every spot, wrinkle, and dull area on a face adds up to several thousand. The total depends on the photo's condition, so you need to pin down "how many spots" at the quoting stage.

Per-person services (Syazou no Takumi's packages, MARVELOUS DESIGN, ASOBOAD, Retouch no Pro, Hito Photo) charge a flat rate per person that covers full-face correction. Since more fixes don't cost more, they can come out cheaper for photos needing a lot of work.

A concrete example: for "5 blemishes + 3 wrinkles + dullness correction," per-spot BELLEFOTO runs ¥500 × 8 spots = from ¥4,000, while per-person MARVELOUS DESIGN covers the whole face with its ¥2,750 soft plan. Flip the case — one blemish only — and BELLEFOTO's ¥500 beats MARVELOUS DESIGN's ¥2,750. When comparing quotes, always confirm what's included at that price.

Five Factors That Move Japanese Retouching Prices

The same "one portrait retouch" can cost anywhere from a few hundred to several thousand yen. Five factors explain the spread.

  • Complexity of the work. Simple color correction, body-shape correction, and complex compositing involve completely different amounts of labor. The same word "retouching" can mean a several-fold difference in unit price.
  • Volume. Bulk orders tend to lower the unit price. MARVELOUS DESIGN's 50-image pack (¥110,000 = ¥2,200/image) is one example; Remage's tiered volume discounts (3% over ¥50,000, 5% over ¥100,000, 10% over ¥150,000) are another.
  • Deadline. Rush jobs usually incur express fees. Ordering with time to spare is the cheapest lever there is.
  • Usage size and medium. Web-use images and large-format print (posters, catalogs) demand different precision. Print work at 350dpi and above exposes flaws, requires more careful work, and costs more. State the intended use and size when ordering.
  • Continuity. Some services discount monthly contracts or repeat orders. If your EC operation needs a steady monthly volume of product images, it's worth negotiating.

Tokyo Retouch's official column also explains that quotes depend on multiple variables — work content, volume, deadline, budget — which is why a single universal market rate is hard to state.

How to Keep Retouching Costs Down

  • Handle simple fixes with smartphone apps. Brightness and light color correction are free in Snapseed or Lightroom Mobile. Reserve paid orders for work you can't do yourself.
  • Use cutout specialists for cutout-only jobs. If background removal is all you need, cutout-specialized services cost far less than retouching services. Eleven of them are compared (from ¥19/image) in the Japanese-language article "背景切り抜き・白抜きの代行・外注費用相場".
  • Test-order before committing. Before a large batch, try 1–3 images to confirm quality and turnaround. If it doesn't work out, the damage is small.
  • Batch photos with the same tendency. One unified instruction ("this series runs dark, raise the contrast") for a batch cuts back-and-forth, time, and cost.

How Volume Discounts Actually Work

Volume discounts exist, but the mechanics differ widely by service. One thing to note: almost no service offers stepwise unit-price reductions for small orders (a few to a few dozen images). Volume discounts generally assume genuinely large orders — on the order of 1,000 images — such as monthly EC product-image processing in the hundreds or thousands, where services offer individual quotes or dedicated plans.

Pricing Small Orders

For a few to a few dozen images, budget at each service's list price without assuming a discount. Some services offer monthly plans or repeat-order discounts, so ask in advance if you'll order regularly.

A Checklist for Not Losing Money on Quotes

Confirm these before accepting a retouching quote:

  • Scope of the base rate. Skin correction only, or body-shape correction too?
  • Conditions for surcharges. What happens if difficulty turns out higher than expected mid-job?
  • Express fees. Whether they exist and how much.
  • Delivery format. Extra fees for formats beyond JPEG (PNG, PSD, TIFF)?
  • Cancellation policy. Fees for cancelling after work begins?

Hidden Costs That Quotes Don't Show

Comparing on unit price alone can leave you paying more in total. Two more things to check:

  • Option surcharges. Whether body correction, background processing, and color correction are included or billed separately. "Portrait retouching" doesn't always include body work or wrinkle removal.
  • Rework cost from bad quality. If the deliverable is unusable, re-ordering elsewhere costs time and money. A test order before the real batch avoids this.

Get Quotes from Multiple Vendors

Because rates vary this much, get quotes from at least two or three services — using the same image and the same work specification so the numbers are actually comparable. Judge responsiveness and deadline reliability alongside price.

Corporate and High-Volume Retouching in Japan

Apparel companies on EC marketplaces and catalog producers that generate hundreds to thousands of retouching jobs a month don't use individual-facing services. They outsource to corporate-specialized retouching companies, for a simple reason: processing large image volumes on short deadlines while keeping quality uniform requires a dedicated structure.

The defining trait of corporate retouching companies is multiple retouchers working to internal regulations. Output stays consistent when the assigned person changes because quality control is systematized. Compared with placing individual orders with freelancers, the per-image price tends to be higher — but once you count the hidden costs of retakes and quality checks, the total can flip in the corporate vendor's favor.

The flip side: corporate services often set minimum lots or monthly contracts and don't suit individuals ordering a few images. For individual orders, Remage, BELLEFOTO, and Graphic Factory are the realistic options.

One misconception worth correcting: "no published rate card = expensive" is wrong. In B2B work, the optimal unit price genuinely changes with volume, work content, and deadline, which forces case-by-case quoting. Services that publish rate cards usually price for small orders; for corporate clients sending real volume, quote-based companies can end up cheaper per image.

Rush Retouching in Japan: Who Can Deliver Tomorrow

"We need it by tomorrow" jobs fail if you pick the wrong vendor. Standard turnaround is 3–5 business days, and plenty of services don't take rush work at all.

There are three realistic options for rush jobs. First, services with explicit same-day or next-day delivery: Motto has delivered in as little as 3 hours, Graphic Factory has a 1-hour rush plan, and MARVELOUS DESIGN's express option delivers within 24 hours at 1.2× the standard rate. Second, freelance retouchers — but whether an individual is available depends entirely on timing. Third, in-store services like Camera no Kitamura, where ID-photo retouching can be picked up in as little as 10 minutes.

That covers a handful of images. Hundreds or thousands of portrait images on a short deadline is a different problem entirely: finishing one image in a few hours and processing a large batch in a few days require completely different structures. Bring a bulk job to a small operation and you get either subcontracted, inconsistent output or a slipped deadline. For portrait retouching in the hundreds to thousands of images, Tokyo Retouch — with about 50 staff retouchers and corporate high-volume rush support — is the fit.

Rush surcharges typically run 1.5–2× the standard rate. If the schedule allows, standard turnaround is cheaper. The cases worth paying express fees for are the ones where being late costs more than the fee: seasonal EC listings, event-deadline banners, and memorial portraits (iei), which in Japan are often ordered only after funeral dates are set. Vendors that handle memorial portraits are compared in the Japanese-language article "遺影写真のレタッチはどこに頼む？依頼先9社比較".

FAQ

Q. What does photo retouching cost in Japan?

A. It depends on the task. Color correction runs ¥500–1,000 per image, portrait retouching ¥1,000–6,000, and compositing from ¥1,500; background removal costs tens to hundreds of yen. Choosing a vendor on price alone is risky — cheap-and-bad operators exist, as do vendors who bill surcharges without warning. Confirm whether they'll do a test job, and pick vendors whose inquiry responses are careful.

Q. Does a higher price mean higher quality?

A. Not reliably. And a vendor calling its own work high quality is not the same as customers rating it highly. For multi-image orders, verify quality with a test job first.

Q. Can individuals order retouching in Japan, or is it all corporate?

A. Many services accept individual orders from a single image: Motto, BELLEFOTO, Remage, Syazou no Takumi, MARVELOUS DESIGN, Graphic Factory, and Hito Photo among them. Tokyo Retouch and Studio Koubou are corporate-only.

Q. What's the market rate for portrait retouching specifically?

A. ¥1,000–6,000 per image covers most of the market. Some services also offer per-spot partial fixes at ¥100–500 per spot. Counting methods differ: BELLEFOTO lets you switch between ¥500 per-spot fixes and a ¥3,000 full-face plan, Remage is per-spot only from ¥550, and Motto adds up itemized fixes (¥100 for a mole/blemish, ¥300 for a wrinkle, ¥600 for a facial cleanup bundle).

Q. How do I keep the cost down?

A. Narrow the work. Brightness and color-level fixes are free with smartphone apps. Cutout-only jobs are far cheaper at cutout specialists (from ¥19/image). Batching images lowers unit prices at many services, and telling the vendor an image is for small web use — not print — can reduce the rate as well.

Q. What's the cheapest option for background removal only?

A. Cutout specialists at ¥19–120 per image. Eleven of them are compared in the Japanese-language article on cutout outsourcing linked above.

Q. How fast can retouching be delivered?

A. Motto in as little as 3 hours, Graphic Factory's rush plan in as little as 1 hour, MARVELOUS DESIGN's express within 24 hours, and Camera no Kitamura's in-store ID-photo retouching in as little as 10 minutes. Crowdsourced orders usually take 1–3 days.

Q. Should I retouch photos myself or hire a pro?

A. Light fixes — brightness, color temperature, a small blemish — are free with apps. Hiring makes sense when you need uniform quality across many images, work beyond your own skill, or your time back.

Q. How do corporate-only and individual-facing services differ?

A. Tokyo Retouch and Studio Koubou serve corporations only and decline individual orders. Motto, BELLEFOTO, Graphic Factory, and Hito Photo accept individuals. For bulk orders, corporate services can come out cheaper per image.

Q. Which retouching vendors do large Japanese companies actually use?

A. Large companies and talent agencies use corporate-specialized retouching companies such as Tokyo Retouch and Studio Koubou, which keep dozens of retouchers on staff and hold quality uniform across bulk jobs. These vendors don't take individual orders; individuals should use services like Remage or Graphic Factory instead.

Q. Freelancer or company — which should I outsource to?

A. Both have merits. Freelancers take single-image orders casually and you can check their style in a portfolio first, but one person means real risk of missed deadlines or sudden silence. Companies offer stable quality and deadlines with multi-person structures and handle volume, but may impose minimum lots or corporate-only policies. Realistically: freelancers for a few one-off images, companies for ongoing volume.

Q. Any vendors for rush corporate jobs?

A. On the corporate side, Tokyo Retouch handles express work. On the individual side, Motto (as fast as 3 hours) and Graphic Factory (as fast as 1 hour) offer rush plans. Expect surcharges of roughly 1.5–2×. For bulk rush jobs of hundreds of images or more, a corporate vendor with real staffing — such as Tokyo Retouch with its ~50-person structure — is the realistic option.

Q. Freelancers keep delivering inconsistent quality. What do I do?

A. The usual cause is an unshared finish standard. Three fixes: supply one reference image and say "match this direction"; make revision requests concrete ("match the skin brightness to this sample," not "a bit brighter"); and test-order the same photo with 2–3 freelancers, compare, then commit to the best one — and tell them you intend to order repeatedly so they prioritize you.

Q. Can the invoice differ from the quote?

A. Yes — typically when the actual work turns out heavier than the image suggested. Confirm the surcharge conditions in advance and, ideally, agree on a price ceiling.

Q. Where should I order blemish and spot removal?

A. BELLEFOTO and Motto take per-spot orders. Motto charges ¥100 per mole/blemish/spot and ¥300 per wrinkle; BELLEFOTO starts at ¥500 per spot and can switch to a ¥3,000 full-face plan. For 1–2 spots these two are cheapest; as the count grows, a full-face plan becomes the better deal.

Q. Best option for a single high-stakes portrait as an individual?

A. Hito Photo. At ¥8,000–10,000 per person it's on the expensive end, but the service is built around hand-finished portrait quality — profile photos and commemorative shots where the one image has to be right.

Q. Best option for ID-photo retouching?

A. Camera no Kitamura's in-store service (¥2,380–9,980, same-day pickup) for speed; Syazou no Takumi online (from ¥2,000/person) with a dedicated job-hunting set. Since an ID photo's impression feeds directly into hiring decisions, this is one category where paying a pro beats a phone app.

Q. Who handles celebrity and talent photos?

A. Tokyo Retouch, at ¥1,000–4,000 per person, serving corporate clients in Japan's entertainment and publishing industries with high-volume, short-deadline capability. It is corporate-only; an individual wanting talent-photo-grade finishing should look at Hito Photo.

Q. Wedding photo retouching — studio vs. couple?

A. Studios and venues outsourcing as businesses use Studio Koubou (¥1,200–5,000/image, corporate-only, with weekend and holiday operation that fits venue delivery schedules). Couples ordering as individuals use MARVELOUS DESIGN (¥2,750–5,500/person), which lets you choose the retouching intensity.

Q. Can old black-and-white photos be colorized?

A. Yes. Among the services compared here, Retouch Ink offers AI colorization of black-and-white photos at ¥4,000–12,000 per person — often used for memorial portraits and old family photos. Pricing depends on the photo's condition, so start with a free quote.

Q. What do per-spot fixes cost for wedding and pre-wedding photos?

A. Roughly ¥500–550 per spot: BELLEFOTO from ¥500 and Remage from ¥550. For full-face correction, MARVELOUS DESIGN's soft plan (¥2,750/person) or Hito Photo (from ¥8,000/person) are the options. Studio Koubou (¥1,200–5,000/image) serves studios and venues only, not couples.

Q. What does retouching for apparel and model photography cost?

A. Skin-only correction runs ¥1,000–2,000 per person; adding body-shape work pushes it to ¥3,000 and up. EC product pages often bundle cutouts with retouching, landing anywhere from hundreds to thousands of yen per image. Apparel companies with steady monthly volume typically outsource to corporate retouching companies with quality-control structures.

Q. How do I judge whether a quoted price is fair?

A. There is no single fair price — it moves with work content, quality, and deadline. Rough anchors: ¥100–500 to remove one spot, ¥1,000–6,000 for full-face skin correction, ¥3,000 and up once body correction or compositing is involved. The reliable test is getting 2–3 quotes for the same image and the same specification. Extreme low-ball offers carry quality and security risk; don't choose on price alone.

Conclusion: The Price Depends on What You Fix, How It's Counted, and How Much You Order

Retouching prices in Japan come down to work content × counting method (per spot or per person) × order volume. A single-spot fix can cost ¥100; a full portrait finish can top ¥10,000. Many services drop unit prices for batches, so the efficient move is to wait until your volume is clear, then get quotes.
